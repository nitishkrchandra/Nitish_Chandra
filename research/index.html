Research

Quantum computation across connected processors.

I study the intersection of fault-tolerant computation, error-correcting codes, quantum networks, and architecture-aware compilation. The overarching goal is to understand how these layers can be designed together for scalable quantum computing.

01

Distributed quantum error correction

I investigate how color codes and quantum LDPC codes, including bivariate bicycle codes, can be partitioned and realized across networked quantum processors. I characterize nonlocal syndrome extraction, logical error rates, and interconnect noise.

Bivariate bicycle codes · Color codes · Network noise

Related paper ↗
02

Fault-tolerant logical operations

My work studies the reliability and resource requirements of logical operations spanning separate modules, including distributed lattice surgery and fault-tolerant constructions with optically networked trapped-ion processors.

Lattice surgery · Logical gates · Trapped-ion modules

Related paper ↗
03

Network-aware compilation and scheduling

I develop architecture-aware approaches to compile logical computations and schedule local and nonlocal operations under limited entanglement, communication latency, and physical resource constraints.

Logical compilation · Network scheduling · Resource optimization

Related paper ↗
04

Quantum networks and secure communication

Earlier and related research studies entanglement distribution, optimal policies for quantum switches, quantum repeaters, and the interaction between secure classical control channels and quantum communication.

Entanglement distribution · Quantum repeaters · Quantum-resistant teleportation

Related paper ↗

Current directions

My ongoing interests include co-designing syndrome-extraction schedules and physical-layer execution on modular trapped-ion and neutral-atom platforms, including transversal operations and code-surgery protocols.
