Research

Toward scalable distributed quantum computing

I work at the interface of quantum error correction, fault-tolerant architectures, and quantum networking. My research examines how the limitations of real interconnects influence both logical reliability and the cost of computation.

01

Distributed quantum error correction

I study distributed realizations of topological and quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes, including color codes and bivariate bicycle codes. The aim is to understand how partitioning and nonlocal syndrome extraction affect decoding, logical error rates, and fault tolerance under realistic network noise.

02

Fault-tolerant logical operations

I investigate logical computation across physically separated quantum processors. This includes distributed lattice surgery and fault-tolerant architectures based on optically networked trapped-ion modules, with particular attention to the impact of nonlocal operations on reliability and resource requirements.

03

Compilation, mapping, and scheduling

I study how fault-tolerant workloads can be mapped across modular processors and how local and remote operations should be scheduled. The key constraints include entanglement availability, network topology, latency, and limited computing and communication resources.

Quantum networks and secure operations

My related research addresses entanglement distribution, quantum switches, quantum repeater architectures, teleportation-based operations, and secure classical control for networked quantum systems.
